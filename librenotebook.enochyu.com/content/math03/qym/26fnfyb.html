Directional Derivatives and Gradients

So far, we discussed about different ways to represent a multivariable function and their partial derivatives. Though they are very important, many of the application of multivariable calculus rises from gradients. Physics and machine learning are two fields that actively uses it! In this note, we will discuss about directional derivatives and gradients.

1.1 Directional Derivatives

Before we get too excited with directional derivatives and gradients, let’s first expand our knowledge that we discussed earlier. Interpreting derivatives of a single variable function geometrically, we can think of it as the slope of the line tangent to the graph at the point. In other words, geometrically thinking, there exists a tangent line at that point if the point is differentiable.

We kinda discussed this earlier as well, but let’s extend that intuition for functions of two variables. For functions of two variables, there are many curves on the graph that passes through the point. In other words, if the function is differentiable at a given point, there should be lines tangents to all the curves on the graph. This is simply the tangent plane of the curve at the point. Now with this intuition, let’s define what a tangent plane is. This will be important to understand directional derivatives!

Definition 1.1.1

Let \(S\) be a surface that is differentiable at \((x_0, y_0, z_0)\). The tangent plane \(H\) to \(S\) at \((x_0, y_0, z_0)\) is the plane that contains \((x_0, y_0, z_0)\) and all the lines tangent to any smooth curve on \(S\) that also passes through \((x_0, y_0, z_0)\).
