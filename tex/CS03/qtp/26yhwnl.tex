\chapter*{Introducing the Notes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Introducing the Notes}

As you may have noticed from my previous notes, I love math and
computer science. These two fields are closely related and often
complement each other. Besides studying them for the sake of
learning necessary contents and digesting them, I like to have fun.
And I don't think programming should be limited to practical uses.
Skills in math and computer science can be used to make your learning
and imagination into something tangible. Something that is fun to
create and use.

I mean where is all the fun if you don't use your skills to make
something you like! Some people I meet say doing such works is a
waste of your time. Those don't help in career, employments,
yada yada yada, and you should spend that time solving more problems
and improving your skills. Well, they are wrong! I really hate it
when I hear that over and over. It's true that ``fun projects'' may
not look impressive on your resume and may not help in employments.
But life isn't all about career and resume. Maybe I do not know
enough as a high school student, but having fun and being motivated
to learn is very important to me.

Why do we learn math and computer science in the first place?
For me, its because I like it. More precisely, I like contemplating on
a problem for a long time and solving them. If I don't like them,
I wouldn't be studying them as much as I am doing right now.
Creating something fun and interesting is also one of my main
motivations for studying them. Using your skills to develop something
fun, even if they are not necessarily useful in real life, is an
incredible benefit from studying math and computer science.

So my point is you should make things you like as well! If you like
building and solving problems, I think we should pursue them no matter
their usefulness. Now back to these notes, there are explanations and
motivations to many of my programs that I had fun building and
learning. They are quite different from my other notes, but enjoy!
